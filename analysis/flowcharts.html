Witness-free verification: how it works

A five-minute tour of the work in analysis/ and verifier/, in six diagram sections. Every number is copied from a committed file under analysis/, and the file is named next to it. Every detection number in sections 1 to 6 is against synthetic tampering of real honest sessions: none of the 21 corpora contains a known real fabricated tool result. The same text and diagrams exist in two forms: analysis/FLOWCHARTS.md (renders on GitHub) and analysis/flowcharts.html.

How to read the numbers: k/n is "flagged out of tried". "488/500 vs 3/500 honest" means the check flagged 488 of 500 tampered sessions and 3 of 500 untouched copies. sw/CC is the SWE-chat Claude Code unit, the largest one we hold.

1. The project in one picture

Agent harnesses record what each tool call returned, and those records are treated as evidence, but nothing checks whether a recorded result could have come from the call it is attached to (analysis/DESIGN.md §1.1). We asked how much of that a log can check about itself, using only fields the agent does not control: provider clocks, billing counters, harness timers and second renderings. We held 21 corpora: 7 before acquisition, plus 14 downloaded, of which 6 were loaded and measured (tbench2, agentcap, pub_cc_hf, pub_trace_commons, pub_codex, glm_tb21) and 8 were not, for time (analysis/CORPUS_INVENTORY.md §0, analysis/MORNING_BRIEF.md §1). Only 5 of the 21 carry provider request ids, the strongest clock we found, and all 5 are one harness talking to one provider (analysis/CORPUS_INVENTORY.md §0). The phases produced a design and a verifier scaffold. The last step tests that strategy against a labeled dataset from the separate swarm workstream (section 5 and the results placeholder in section 6).

2. Phases A to E, with their gates

Each corpus's sessions were drawn into disjoint splits: A and B by stratified sampling at the start (analysis/lib/sample.py), E and H in commit 49f254b, before any Phase E measurement (analysis/PHASE_E_PLAN.md).

Phase A only looked at which fields exist and at what resolution, on split A, and its gate killed lines whose data could not carry them, for example latency in aiv_cu, where a call and its result share one stamp in 6,107/6,107 pairs (analysis/PHASE_A.md A1). Thresholds for Phase B were then frozen in a pre-registration commit (3df8d28) from split A alone, and an independent re-derivation reproduced 49 of 50 Phase B unit checks (analysis/FINDINGS.md, "Read this first"). Phase C's 14 discovery lenses read split B before the Phase B probes ran, so B is not blind for anything Phase C found; a skeptic reproduced 200 of its 239 observations (analysis/FINDINGS.md §4.1). Phase E re-ran every survivor and the five Phase D proposals under a second pre-registration (55fc556), using a fresh split E as the replication; split E holds 1,825 swechat and 2,000 aiv_cu sessions (analysis/PREREG_E.md §0). Of 25 candidate x unit rows, 9 survived, 7 were downgraded, 3 were killed and 6 could not be run (analysis/SECOND_PASS.md §1, analysis/out/phase_e/second_pass.json counts). Split H is held out and is read once, after the code freeze (analysis/DESIGN.md §5.2).

3. Hidden witnesses inside ordinary logs, and the attacks they catch

Each witness is a field the agent does not write: the provider mints request ids with a clock inside them, bills output tokens, and the harness stamps time and writes a second structured copy of some results. Against synthetic attacks on sw/CC split E, the request-id clock flags a 30 s back-dated response in 488/500 sessions against 3/500 honest copies, an adjacent id swap in 486/500 and a nearest-time foreign id in 395/500 (analysis/out/phase_e/n7_timing.json cells, keys swechat/claude_code|E|R1_BRACKET|...), with an honest stream rate of 11/3,325 = 0.00331 on E (analysis/SECOND_PASS.md §4.1). The usage ledger catches deleted responses, 396/500 against 4/500, but its E split is not blind, and output-token accounting is the only shipped check with power against an attack inside the threat model, inline fabrication, where it is only partial: 224/500 against 83/500 (analysis/out/phase_e/n7_content.json cells; analysis/DESIGN.md §1.5). Output substitution, consistent rewrites, reordering, deleted pairs and inserted pairs walk through: 10 of 21 attack types have no detecting cell in any detector, unit or split (analysis/SECOND_PASS.md §6). A shim that returns plausible output, and any coherent forgery, passes by construction (analysis/DESIGN.md §1.2, §1.4).

4. Verifier data flow

Every input, whatever its format, becomes the same event table (the IR in analysis/lib/ir.py), and one session's events become a Session that records which fields this log actually carries (analysis/DESIGN.md §2.3). Each check is a plugin that declares the fields it needs; if they are missing, every call gets unconstrained with the reason, so a gap in the data is reported as lost coverage, never as a detection. Composition is a fixed order, not a vote: any contradicted wins, then supported, then unconstrained, and a statistical check may contradict but never support (analysis/DESIGN.md §4.6, DA2). The scaffold runs end to end with the reference check only: 115 tests ran and passed with 1 slow test skipped, and the demo session gives 17/17 calls unconstrained because no detection check is enabled yet (analysis/out/phase_e/scaffold_check.json tests; analysis/out/phase_e/demo/demo_meta.json). The planned checks, in build order, are id_bracket and inline_output_accounting (MUST), ledger_tally and dual_render_recount (SHOULD) and latency_floor (COULD) (analysis/DESIGN.md §7.2).

5. Evaluation protocol

5a. How the corpus measurements were run

Every rule and threshold was written down and committed before the data that judges it was read: thresholds come from split A, and the first measurement is on B, with E as the replication (analysis/PREREG_E.md §0). A check decides on a unit only if its honest leg passes the pre-registered bar there, a rate of at most 0.05 with a CI high of at most 0.10 (analysis/DESIGN.md DA16). Against an attack, DETECTS needs recall of at least 0.5 and a recall CI low above the honest CI high; separation with lower recall is PARTIAL (analysis/out/phase_e/n7_content.json, deciding_number.rule in each cell). Results are reported per unit and per threat class, never as one pooled number (analysis/DESIGN.md DA12). unconstrained is an answer, not a failure, and the share of calls that get it is reported as coverage (analysis/DESIGN.md §1.3).

5b. How the swarm labeled dataset is tested

The swarm dataset is an outside input with labels, so it gets its own split: a session goes to dev when the sha256 of its id is even and to test when it is odd, and labels are looked at on dev only. A rule is kept only if it fires on zero honest calls, checked against every honest real executed call and every honest swarm tool result, because one false contradicted makes the tool useless. Rules and thresholds are frozen into a JSON before the test split is scored. Detectors never read the real output, host stdout or the labels; those are joined only when scoring, and unconstrained counts as an abstention, not a miss. Results are reported per stratum (source, spoof class, unit, whether the agent was told to lie, consistency) and separately for the honest classes that look like positives, such as an agent quoting a false value in order to refute it.

6. Results at a glance

Three candidates survived every attempt to kill them, all in sw/CC: the request-id bracket (R1, replicated on a blind E), the usage ledger (R5, E not blind) and the zero-error tail (P3a, a baseline with no positive control) (analysis/SECOND_PASS.md §1, §8). R2 died on its honest rate, 44/807 = 0.0545 against a 0.05 budget; the pooled round-number row collapsed without one model; and in sw/CC the reaction-time test and five of the seven N5 items are DEAD (analysis/SECOND_PASS.md §7). Across the transfer grid only 87 of 396 loaded cells could be decided, and 7 are ALIVE, down from 19 before the artifact checks and kill tests: most cells are undecidable rather than failed (analysis/SECOND_PASS.md §6, analysis/MORNING_BRIEF.md §2, analysis/out/phase_e/n6_grid.json). Read plainly, the verifier we can ship mostly detects naive record editing; its only in-scope power is partial, against inline fabrication, and a coherent forgery passes (analysis/DESIGN.md §1.5). We do not claim the request-id bracket method: prior art for it exists (analysis/PRIOR_ART.md, B2 rows).

Swarm dataset results

We tested five frozen checks on the test split of the swarm team's labeled dataset, scoring it once. Each check fired on 0 of 179,365 honest tool calls and on 0 of 10,000 honest test rows. On fabricated tool outputs the combined verdict contradicts 3,778 of 5,948 (0.635), against 368 (0.062) for the baseline. On fabricated claims it contradicts 144 of 164 (0.878), against 38 (0.232) for the baseline. The union with the baseline reaches 4,041 (0.679) and 145 (0.884), still with no false positives. Two numbers are close to built in: injected_I-struct 780/780 (T0) and injected_I-recompute 743/744 (T1), because the injector creates those two classes from the very invariants these checks test.

What stays out of reach is the coherent forgery. On test, ours abstains on 2,169 of the 5,948 fabricated tool outputs, mostly counterfactual (834 of 1,149) and tiered (823 of 1,465) rows whose output cannot be recomputed from the transcript and whose token count is within tolerance. This dataset cannot test the request-id clock (vLLM ids carry no time) or the timing mechanisms (forged rows keep the original timing).
